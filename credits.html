Acknowledgement

What inspired the animation

The feel of the front page — a slow flight through a sparse field, seen from something moving through it, with objects trailing ribbons behind them — was inspired by Paper Planes (paperplanes.world), made by Active Theory for Google and launched on Peace Day 2016.

No code, assets, geometry, shaders, textures or copy from that project are used here. It is a separate work by other people, and its rights are theirs. Everything on this site was written from scratch: the scene, the flight path, the materials and the subject matter are my own, and the subject matter — a quantum circuit rather than paper planes — is different in the first place. The acknowledgement is here because credit for an idea is owed even when nothing is copied.

References

What the animation depicts

The front page draws a real algorithm, so it is worth citing properly.

  • 1997

    P. W. Shor

    Polynomial-Time Algorithms for Prime Factorization and Discrete Logarithms on a Quantum Computer. SIAM Journal on Computing 26(5), 1484–1509.

    Preprint: arXiv:quant-ph/9508027

  • 2010

    M. A. Nielsen & I. L. Chuang

    Quantum Computation and Quantum Information, 10th anniversary edition, Cambridge University Press. The circuit conventions used in the animation — control dots, the boxed modular exponentiation, the inverse QFT, meter symbols — follow §5.3.

Build

How the page is made

Hand-written HTML and CSS, no framework and no build step. The CV PDF is produced from LaTeX with latexmk. Deployment is a plain file copy.

If the animation is not running, that is deliberate: it stops when the tab is hidden or the hero is scrolled out of view, and it never starts if your system asks for reduced motion or WebGL is unavailable — you get a static drawing of the same circuit instead. You can force that drawing with ?static=1.
