Title

From Graph Isomorphism/Automorphism to Tensor Isomorphism/Automorphism

Goal

My current task is to understand the graph-side relationships between GI, GA, #GI, and #GA from Köbler--Schöning--Torán, and then identify which parts of this workflow may have tensor analogues.

1. Graph-Side Definitions

For graphs G and H, the graph isomorphism problem asks whether there exists a bijection between their vertex sets preserving adjacency. I write

GI={(G,H):G≅H}.

The automorphism group of a graph G is

Aut⁡(G)={φ∈Sn:φ(G)=G}.

The graph automorphism problem asks whether Aut⁡(G) contains a non-identity element.

The corresponding counting versions are

#GI(G,H)=|Iso⁡(G,H)|,

and

#GA(G)=|Aut⁡(G)|.

2. Reduction from GA to GI

For a vertex i∈V(G), let G[i] denote the graph G with vertex i distinguished or labelled.

Then G has a nontrivial automorphism if and only if there exist distinct vertices i≠j such that

G[i]≅G[j].

Indeed, if an automorphism φ∈Aut⁡(G) moves i to j, then φ gives an isomorphism

G[i]≅G[j].

Conversely, any isomorphism

G[i]≅G[j]

with i≠j induces a nontrivial automorphism of G.

Therefore GA can be decided using polynomially many GI-queries.

3. The Disjoint-Union Trick

For connected graphs G and H,

G≅H

if and only if the disjoint union G⊔H has an automorphism that switches the two connected components.

This is the key graph-side mechanism:

isomorphism between two objects⇝symmetry of their disjoint union.

4. Counting Relation

For connected graphs G and H, one has

#GA(G⊔H)=#GA(G)#GA(H)+#GI(G,H)2.

If G≇H, then there are no automorphisms switching the two connected components, so every automorphism of G⊔H preserves each component.

If G≅H, then the additional automorphisms are exactly those that switch the two components. These are determined by choosing an isomorphism G→H and an isomorphism H→G, giving the #GI(G,H)2 term.

5. Tensor Analogue

For tensors

A,B∈U⊗V⊗W,

the tensor isomorphism problem asks whether there exists

(P,Q,R)∈GL(U)×GL(V)×GL(W)

such that

B=(P,Q,R)⋅A.

The automorphism group of A is

Aut⁡(A)={(P,Q,R):(P,Q,R)⋅A=A}.

The natural graph-to-tensor analogy is

G⊔H⇝A⊕︎B.

The desired tensor-side statement would be something like:

A≅B

if and only if

A⊕︎B

has an automorphism that switches the two direct summands.

6. Main Obstruction

In graph theory, the connected components of G⊔H are canonical and combinatorially visible.

In tensor theory, the direct summands of A⊕︎B are not necessarily visible after arbitrary changes of basis. Therefore, the tensor analogue cannot rely only on the formal expression A⊕︎B. It requires a theorem ensuring that direct-sum decompositions are sufficiently canonical.

This is where a Strassen/Krull--Schmidt type direct-sum decomposition theorem may be needed.

Informally:

graph connected components⇝tensor indecomposable summands.

The difficulty is that tensor summands are linear-algebraic objects rather than visibly separated combinatorial components.

7. Questions

  1. Are we trying to prove

    TI≤pTA,

    or

    TI≤pcTA,

    or are we only trying to identify the obstruction to such a reduction?

  2. What is the correct tensor category for the analogue?

    Possible candidates include:

    • ordinary 3-tensors;

    • bilinear maps;

    • structure tensors of algebras or rings.

  3. Which version of Strassen's indecomposable direct-sum theorem is actually needed?

  4. If Aut⁡(A⊕︎B) is given by generators, how do we detect whether the generated group contains an element that switches the two direct summands?

  5. More generally, what is the correct tensor-side analogue of the graph operation of distinguishing, colouring, or labelling vertices?
